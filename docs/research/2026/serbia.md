# Serbia employee payroll, income year 2026 — clean-room research dossier

**Scope and access date.** This dossier was independently reconstructed from Serbian government, Tax Administration (PURS), Central Registry (CROSO), official legal-information-system, and Ministry of Finance material, accessed **2026-10-04**. It models a Serbian-resident employee, age 30 on 31 December 2026, single, no children, full-time ordinary private employment, and twelve equal monthly payments of regular gross cash salary. No employer incentive, refund, special sector rule, benefit in kind, other income, or voluntary deduction is assumed.

All amounts are Serbian dinars (RSD). “Gross” below means Serbian gross salary before employee payroll tax and employee social contributions (“gross 1”). Employer contributions are added to it (“gross 2”).

## 1. Sources and evidential pinpoints

### Primary legislation and official publications

1. **Ministry of Finance, “Zakon o porezu na dohodak građana” (Personal Income Tax Law), current-law landing page.** The page lists the consolidated act through *Official Gazette RS* 19/2025, 109/2025 and 80/2026 (page lines headed by the full act title), plus the 2026 dinar-amount notices. In the act, the operative salary pinpoints are art. 15a (monthly non-taxable amount/full-time adjustment) and art. 16 (10% salary-tax rate). <https://mfin.gov.rs/propisi/zakon-o-porezu-na-dohodak-gradjana>. This is the controlling law index for the salary and annual-tax rules used below.

2. **Ministry of Finance, “Mali: Vlada u četvrtak donosi odluku o redovnom povećanju minimalne zarade na 551 evro od 1. januara 2026. godine”, published 9 September 2025.** The final paragraph on the non-taxable amount says that, from 1 January 2026, it is increased to **RSD 34,221**. <https://mfin.gov.rs/sr/aktivnosti-1/mali-vlada-u-etvrtak-donosi-odluku-o-redovnom-poveanju-minimalne-zarade-na-551-evro-od-1-januara-2026-godine-1>.

3. **Tax Administration, “Poreski informator za fizička lica koja obavljaju samostalnu delatnost”, January 2025, p. 31, payroll example/rate table.** Although the booklet is addressed to entrepreneurs, p. 31 separately states the current employee-payroll rates: salary tax **10%**; pension/disability (PIO) **14% employee + 10% employer**; health **5.15% + 5.15%**; unemployment **0.75% employee**. It also states the deduction order in the worked payroll calculation. <https://purs.gov.rs/upload/media/2025/3/7/759138/Poreski_informator_za_fizicka_lica_koja_obavljaju_samostalnu_delatnost_jan_2025.pdf>.

4. **Ministry of Finance, “Zakon o doprinosima za obavezno socijalno osiguranje” (Mandatory Social Insurance Contributions Law), current-law landing page.** The page lists the consolidated law through *Official Gazette RS* 109/2025 and 80/2026 and, at page lines 155–160 under the act, the three 2026 base notices published in *Official Gazette RS* 112/2025 on 12 December 2025: **“Iznos najniže mesečne osnovice … za 2026”, “Iznos najviše mesečne osnovice … za 2026”, and “Iznos najviše godišnje osnovice … za 2026.”** <https://mfin.gov.rs/sr/propisi-1/zakon-o-doprinosima-za-obavezno-socijalno-osiguranje-1>. Their published values are respectively **RSD 51,297 per month**, **RSD 732,820 per month**, and **RSD 8,793,840 per year**, effective 1 January–31 December 2026.

5. **CROSO, “Osnovice doprinosa” (Contribution bases).** The official registry’s explanation pinpoints Contributions Law arts. 37 and 43: the minimum monthly base is 35% of the relevant average monthly wage, and the maximum annual base is five times the relevant average annual wage. <https://www.croso.gov.rs/cir/Statistika/Osnovice_doprinosa/>. CROSO’s glossary entry **“Najviša mesečna osnovica doprinosa”** explains that the monthly maximum replaces a prescribed base that is higher and is five times the prescribed average monthly wage. <https://www.croso.gov.rs/lat/Recnik/index.php?slovo=N>.

6. **Tax Administration-hosted “Zakon o doprinosima za obavezno socijalno osiguranje”, arts. 37–43.** Article 37 governs the minimum; art. 41 says that where the prescribed base exceeds the maximum monthly base, contributions are calculated on the maximum; art. 42 defines that maximum as five times the referenced average wage. <https://www.purs.gov.rs/upload/media/2025/2/4/417359/Zakonodoprinosimazaobaveznosocijalnoosiguranje.pdf>.

7. **Official Legal Information System, current consolidated “Zakon o porezu na dohodak građana”, arts. 87–89.** Article 87 sets the annual personal-income-tax entry threshold at **three average annual salaries**, measures the covered income net of paid tax and compulsory employee contributions, and gives a taxpayer under 40 at year-end an additional reduction of **three average annual salaries**, no greater than the qualifying income. Article 88 gives the taxpayer personal deduction of **15% of the average annual salary** and caps total personal deductions at 50% of income for taxation. Article 89 taxes the base at **10% up to six average annual salaries** and **15% above that**. <https://reg.pravno-informacioni-sistem.rs/api/viewdoc?doctype=reg&regactid=438284&uuid=8d7c110b-252e-41b2-bff9-35a15a22dc37>.

8. **Tax Administration, “Objašnjenje u vezi sa godišnjim porezom za 2025. godinu”, published April 2026, especially §6.** This official guidance confirms the operational reading of art. 87: under-40 status is tested on the last day of the calendar year and qualifying income, already reduced by paid taxes and employee contributions, is further reduced by three average annual salaries. The document concerns 2025 and is used only to interpret the enduring mechanism, not as a source of a 2026 threshold. <https://purs.gov.rs/upload/media/2026/4/1/760672/Obja%C5%A1njenje_u_vezi_sa_godi%C5%A1njim_porezom_za_2025._godinu.pdf>.

9. **Tax Administration, “Rok za podnošenje prijave i plaćanje godišnjeg poreza na dohodak građana ističe 15. maja”, 5 May 2026.** It confirms the annual tax is handled through the pre-filled electronic return and is filed and paid by **15 May of the following year**. <https://purs.gov.rs/lat/odnosi-s-javnoscu/Strana2/Strana6/4608558/rok-za-podnosenje-prijave-i-placanje-godisnjeg-poreza-na-dohodak-gradjana-istice-15-maja.html>.

10. **Tax Administration, “Najniže mesečne osnovice za plaćanje doprinosa”, 18 January 2023.** This official historical table and explanation confirms the statutory publication mechanism and that the annual minimum applies from 1 January of the stated year. <https://www.purs.gov.rs/aktuelnosti/Ostalo/608/najnize-mesecne-osnovice-za-placanje-doprinosa.html>. It is corroborative methodology, not the source of the 2026 amount.

## 2. Universal ordinary-payroll rules used

For a full month of work, let monthly gross salary be `g`.

### Salary tax

The monthly salary-tax base is

```text
Tbase_month = max(0, g - 34,221)
salary_tax_month = 10% × Tbase_month
```

The RSD 34,221 allowance is monthly, not an annual free-standing deduction. For twelve equal full-month salaries it is reproducibly annualised to **RSD 410,652**.

### Compulsory social contributions

For the assumed ordinary employee, the 2026 monthly contribution base is

```text
Cbase_month = min(max(g, 51,297), 732,820)
```

Applied to that base:

| Component | Employee | Employer |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pension and disability (PIO) | 14.00% | 10.00% |
| Health insurance | 5.15% | 5.15% |
| Unemployment | 0.75% | 0.00% |
| **Total** | **19.90%** | **15.15%** |

Thus:

```text
employee_contributions = 19.90% × Cbase
employer_contributions = 15.15% × Cbase
cash_net_before_annual_tax = gross - salary_tax - employee_contributions
employer_cost = gross + employer_contributions
```

The monthly maximum matters even if annual gross is known: a bonus concentrated in one month is not equivalent to twelve equal payments. For the expressly assumed twelve equal salaries, twelve monthly maxima equal the published annual maximum: `12 × 732,820 = 8,793,840`. The annual maximum is principally relevant to annual reconciliation/refund and aggregation across payers; it does not authorize an employer to ignore monthly caps.

No sample falls below the monthly minimum. A low-pay example could produce contributions based on more than cash gross; the minimum is therefore not a deduction from gross but a floor on the contribution base.

## 3. Supplementary annual personal-income tax

This is a separate self-assessed liability, not an extra payroll withholding in the ordinary monthly calculation.

Let:

- `A` = the **official average annual salary for calendar 2026**, when published by the competent statistics authority;
- `N` = covered annual employment income after salary tax and employee compulsory contributions (the “cash net before annual tax” calculated above).

For this age-30, no-dependent case, arts. 87–89 reduce to:

```text
Q = max(0, N - 6A)
    # 3A entry threshold plus the under-40 reduction of up to 3A

D = min(0.15A, 0.50Q)
    # taxpayer personal deduction; no dependent deduction

B = max(0, Q - D)

supplementary_annual_tax S(A)
  = 0.10 × min(B, 6A)
  + 0.15 × max(0, B - 6A)

final_net = N - S(A)
```

**Unresolved at the access date:** calendar-2026 `A` cannot be known on 2026-10-04 because the year is incomplete. Consequently the exact 2026 annual-tax threshold and exact tax at each salary cannot yet be stated from an official source. Substituting a prior-year statistic would silently calculate the wrong statutory year. The worked results therefore give exact ordinary payroll and an exact symbolic annual-tax expression to finish after publication.

## 5. Bonuses, “13th salary”, reliefs, and boundaries

- **Bonus/13th payment.** Serbia has no separate universal 13th-salary tax regime used here. Cash paid by reason of employment that has the character of salary belongs in salary payroll. It attracts the 10% salary tax after the applicable monthly exemption treatment and contributions subject to that payment month’s cumulative minimum/maximum mechanics. A bonus paid in one month can therefore yield different current contributions from spreading identical annual cash evenly. Any later annual contribution reconciliation/refund must be handled separately. Because this dossier assumes twelve equal regular payments, no bonus timing rule is embedded in the numeric table.

- **Employer reliefs.** The Income Tax Law and Contributions Law contain targeted new-hire/employer refund or exemption regimes (the Ministry current-law pages show the 109/2025 and 80/2026 amendments). Those are not universal rate reductions: entitlement depends on facts such as the employer and employee category, registration/unemployment history, headcount increase, qualifying dates, and state-aid limits. None of those facts is present in the assumed ordinary employment, so **no relief or refund is credited**. This is deliberate; it prevents presenting a conditional subsidy as a statutory employer rate.

- **Not employer payroll charges.** The supplementary annual personal-income tax is the individual’s later annual liability. It does not increase employer cost in the tables.

- **Minimum-base boundary.** The RSD 51,297 minimum can apply even when monthly salary is lower, subject to statutory proportional and multi-employer rules. Those cases are outside the full-time, single-employer samples.

- **Other employment costs.** Contractual benefits, severance, occupational-safety expense, collective-agreement entitlements and payroll administration are not percentage payroll taxes and are not added. No universal employer levy beyond the 10% PIO and 5.15% health contributions was identified for this ordinary case.

## 6. Evidence assessment

**Strongest evidence.** The contribution architecture is unusually well triangulated: the Ministry’s current-law page identifies the exact 2026 *Official Gazette* base notices; the Tax Administration’s legislation and rate table give the cap mechanism and split rates; and CROSO independently explains the statutory base multipliers. The annual personal-income-tax formula is directly traceable to arts. 87–89 of the official consolidated act and PURS’s worked interpretive guidance.

**Weakest evidence / items to refresh.** The official 2026 base amounts are exposed through the Ministry’s index to *Official Gazette* 112/2025 rather than a conveniently machine-readable Ministry table. More importantly, the 2026 average annual salary required for the supplementary annual tax is inherently unavailable before calendar-year completion. That annual-tax layer must be refreshed after the official statistic and the Tax Administration’s 2026 annual-tax guidance are published in 2027. Bonus timing and employer relief eligibility are fact-specific; neither should be inferred from annual gross alone.
