# Norway employee payroll and income tax, 2026 — independent research dossier

**All sources accessed:** 2026-10-04
**Currency:** Norwegian kroner (NOK)
**Result type:** final 2026 annual tax liability and a statutory employer-cost floor, not a reproduction of tax-card withholding by pay period.

## 1. Reproducible case and scope

The employee is age 40 (therefore born outside the 1991–2006 cohort for the randomized youth work-deduction trial), resident in Norway throughout 2026, single, under pension age, with no children, wealth tax, debt interest, union dues, travel expense, gifts, other income, or deductions. The employee is an ordinary member of the National Insurance Scheme and is not taxed under PAYE (`kildeskatt på lønn`).

The employer is an ordinary private-sector, non-agricultural business registered in **Oslo**. Oslo is chosen because it is unambiguously in residual employer-contribution **zone I**, making the rate 14.1% without a zone-Ia de-minimis calculation. A location-free Norwegian employer cost is not reproducible because the 2026 ordinary rates range from 0% to 14.1%.

`G` means total regular taxable gross cash received in 2026, including any holiday pay actually paid in 2026 and after any salary deduction made for vacation. It excludes employer pension contributions and insurance premiums. This definition is essential: holiday pay earned in 2026 is normally paid in 2027, and a quoted contractual annual salary does not by itself reveal the taxable 2026 cash after holiday-pay and vacation-deduction timing.

The employer has a minimum defined-contribution occupational pension arrangement: 2% from the first krone through 12 G. No employee pension contribution, AFP scheme, collective-agreement pension, or benefit in kind is assumed.

### Holiday pay, occupational pension, and insurance

11. **Lov 29. april 1988 nr. 21 om ferie (ferieloven)**, section 10: <https://lovdata.no/lov/1988-04-29-21>. Pinpoints: holiday-pay basis is work remuneration paid in the accrual year, excluding holiday pay itself and listed non-wage items; statutory holiday pay is **10.2%**. For an employee over 60 the rate rises 2.3 percentage points, with the increment limited above 6 G. The selected employee is 40. Arbeidstilsynet, **“Feriepenger”**, explains that 12% normally accompanies a collectively agreed fifth week and is contractual rather than the statutory minimum: <https://www.arbeidstilsynet.no/arbeidstid-og-organisering/ferie/feriepenger/>.

12. Skatteetaten, **“Feriepenger”**: <https://www.skatteetaten.no/person/skatt/hjelp-til-riktig-skatt/arbeid-trygd-og-pensjon/feriepenger>. Pinpoint: holiday pay is taxable income in the payment year; qualifying next-year payment may be free of withholding, but is not tax-free. Skatteetaten's 2026 advance-assessment guidance says annual withholding is loaded into the other 10.5 months to accommodate holiday pay and half withholding before Christmas.

13. **Lov 21. desember 2005 nr. 124 om obligatorisk tjenestepensjon (OTP-loven)**, last material minimum-rule amendment effective **1 January 2022**, section 4: <https://lovdata.no/nav/lov/2005-12-21-124>. Pinpoints: minimum annual defined contribution is **2% of salary up to 12 G**; the employer must additionally pay the premium for waiver of contributions on disability and the arrangement's administration costs. Skatteetaten's **“OTP – Obligatorisk tjenestepensjon”** confirms first-day coverage and the two-percent minimum: <https://www.skatteetaten.no/bedrift-og-organisasjon/arbeidsgiver/otp---obligatorisk-tjenestepensjon/>.

14. NAV, **“Grunnbeløpet i folketrygden”**, effective **1 May 2026**: <https://www.nav.no/grunnbelopet>. Pinpoints: G is NOK 136,549 from 1 May 2026 and the official average G for 2026 is **NOK 134,419**. The annual model uses average G, so `12 G = 12 × 134,419 = NOK 1,613,028`.

15. **Lov 16. juni 1989 nr. 65 om yrkesskadeforsikring**, section 3: <https://lovdata.no/nav/lov/1989-06-16-65/%C2%A73>. Pinpoint: every employer must purchase private occupational-injury insurance covering occupational injury and disease. The statute fixes coverage, not a universal premium; the premium therefore cannot be derived from gross salary alone.

## 4. Employee calculation order

For salary-only facts:

```text
minimum-standard deduction M = min(46% × G, 95,700)
ordinary-income base A       = max(G - M - 114,540, 0)
ordinary-income tax          = 22% × A
```

The 2026 bracket tax is levied separately on gross personal income `G`:

| Slice of `G` | Rate |
|---:|---:|
| 0–226,100 | 0% |
| 226,100–318,300 | 1.7% |
| 318,300–725,050 | 4.0% |
| 725,050–980,100 | 13.7% |
| 980,100–1,467,200 | 16.8% |
| Over 1,467,200 | 17.8% |

Employee National Insurance is:

```text
NI = 0                                                   if G < 99,650
NI = min(7.6% × G, 25% × (G - 99,650))                  otherwise
```

The taper protects income just above the lower threshold. The two expressions cross at about NOK 143,175; all five examples are above that point and therefore use 7.6% of all `G`. There is no upper contribution ceiling for this ordinary employee.

```text
total employee liability = ordinary-income tax + bracket tax + NI
take-home cash            = G - total employee liability
```

These are annual liabilities. A tax card determines advance withholding and will not reproduce the same amount in every month, particularly because holiday pay may be paid without contemporaneous withholding.

## 6. Employer statutory floor

For the chosen Oslo employer:

```text
OTP saving             = 2% × min(G, 1,613,028)
employer NI on salary  = 14.1% × G
employer NI on OTP     = 14.1% × OTP saving
quantified cost floor  = G + OTP saving + both employer-NI amounts
```

This is a floor, not the employer's complete invoice. OTP administration, mandatory disability contribution-waiver premium, occupational-injury insurance premium, and any employer National Insurance that applies to those pension premiums must be added, but no official universal amount exists.

| `G` | Minimum OTP saving | Employer NI on `G` | Employer NI on OTP | Quantified cost floor |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200,000 | 4,000.00 | 28,200.00 | 564.00 | 232,764.00 + variable premiums |
| 600,000 | 12,000.00 | 84,600.00 | 1,692.00 | 698,292.00 + variable premiums |
| 1,000,000 | 20,000.00 | 141,000.00 | 2,820.00 | 1,163,820.00 + variable premiums |
| 2,000,000 | 32,260.56 | 282,000.00 | 4,548.74 | 2,318,809.30 + variable premiums |
| 6,000,000 | 32,260.56 | 846,000.00 | 4,548.74 | 6,882,809.30 + variable premiums |

The OTP ceiling is the only high-income cap in this employer floor. The former extra 5% employer contribution above a high-salary threshold does **not** apply in 2026.

## 7. Holiday-pay treatment and timing

Holiday pay is deferred replacement for salary during vacation, not automatically an addition to a stated annual salary. It is earned on qualifying remuneration in one year and usually paid in the next. Holiday pay itself is excluded from its own accrual base.

If, unlike `G`, each example were a pure 2026 holiday-pay-eligible wage base `B`, the statutory minimum obligation earned for later payment would be:

| `B` | 10.2% holiday pay earned |
|---:|---:|
| 200,000 | 20,400.00 |
| 600,000 | 61,200.00 |
| 1,000,000 | 102,000.00 |
| 2,000,000 | 204,000.00 |
| 6,000,000 | 612,000.00 |

Those amounts are not added to the worked `G`: by definition `G` already contains the holiday pay actually received and reflects any vacation salary deduction. When holiday pay is paid it is taxable and subject to employer National Insurance, even if no advance tax is withheld at that moment. A first employment year, termination year, change of employer, or contractual fifth week requires a cash-flow calculation across years.

## 8. Age and other special rules

- The selected age 40 avoids the 5.1% National Insurance rate for persons under 17 or over 69, the additional holiday-pay percentage and extra holiday from age 60, and the randomized youth work deduction.
- Salary remains subject to 7.6% employee National Insurance without an upper cap. High income instead encounters the fifth bracket-tax band and the 12 G cap on minimum OTP saving.
- Finnmark/North Troms residents can have an 18.5% ordinary-income rate and separate regional relief. None is used for the Oslo-resident case.
- Wealth tax is not a payroll calculation and cannot be inferred from salary. It is excluded by the no-wealth assumption.

## 9. Evidence assessment and unresolved facts

**Strongest evidence.** The employee rates and thresholds are reproduced consistently in the enacted 2026 Storting resolutions, the Finance Ministry's final 2026 rate table, and Skatteetaten's 2026 advance-assessment and Tax ABC pages. The 2026 employer zones and rates are directly stated in the official 2026 proposal/resolution materials, and Skatteetaten expressly confirms abolition of the extra high-salary employer contribution.

**Weakest evidence / unavoidable variability.** The statutes require occupational-injury cover, OTP administration, and a disability contribution-waiver benefit, but official law does not set universal premiums. Prices depend on insurer, headcount, occupation/risk, age mix, and arrangement. Related employer National Insurance can therefore also vary. These amounts are shown as unresolved additions rather than guessed.

The use of **average 2026 G** to express the annual 12 G OTP ceiling is transparent and reproducible from NAV's official figure. In live payroll, a pension provider may apply the G value effective during each contribution period and reconcile after the 1 May change; provider-period rounding can differ by a few kroner from the annualized figure.

Holiday-pay basis and payout cannot be reverse-engineered from annual taxable cash alone. Contractual fifth-week holiday pay (normally 12%), AFP participation, pension saving above the 2% minimum, employee pension contributions, collective insurance, benefits, and payroll service fees are all employer/contract-specific and excluded. No value is guessed for them.
