# Ireland employee payroll and employer cost, 2026 — independent research dossier

**All sources accessed:** 2026-10-04
**Currency:** euro (EUR)
**Result type:** annual PAYE/USC plus pay-period PRSI and MyFutureFund deductions for regular cash salary.

## 1. Reproducible case and scope

The employee is Irish-resident, single, age **30**, has no children, no other income, benefits, deductions, medical card, pension arrangement, or Revenue adjustments, and works throughout 2026 in ordinary private employment insured at PRSI Class A. Age 30 is an explicit modelling assumption because MyFutureFund automatic eligibility is age-dependent. Absence of an existing payroll pension is also explicit: an employment already covered by a qualifying payroll pension is excluded from MyFutureFund.

Salary `G` is paid in **52 equal Friday payrolls**, beginning 2 January and ending 25 December 2026. This produces 39 pays before 1 October and 13 pays from 1 October. The convention is needed because PRSI is pay-period based and its rates change on 1 October, while MyFutureFund stops only after the payroll in which cumulative gross breaches EUR 80,000. A monthly or irregular payroll can therefore produce a different MyFutureFund total and cent-level PRSI differences from the same annual salary.

The figures below calculate with unrounded intermediate values and round displayed annual totals to cents. Production payroll rounds each pay run and may include a balancing final salary payment, so small cent differences are expected.

## 3. Calculation rules and order

### PAYE Income Tax

For this full-year employee:

```text
gross Income Tax = 20% × min(G, 44,000)
                 + 40% × max(G - 44,000, 0)

PAYE = max(gross Income Tax - 2,000 Single Person Credit
                            - 2,000 Employee Credit, 0)
```

There is no general employee social-insurance deduction from the Income Tax base. MyFutureFund does not reduce taxable pay: its employee contribution is taken from already-taxed net pay.

### USC

If annual USC-able income is at most EUR 13,000, USC is zero. Otherwise:

```text
USC = 0.5% × min(G, 12,012)
    + 2.0% × min(max(G - 12,012, 0), 16,688)
    + 3.0% × min(max(G - 28,700, 0), 41,344)
    + 8.0% × max(G - 70,044, 0)
```

All five worked salaries exceed the exemption. No medical-card or age-70 reduced rate applies.

### Class A PRSI

Let weekly pay `W = G / 52`. For a week in the tapered band:

```text
credit(W) = 12 - (W - 352.01) / 6, for 352.01 <= W <= 424
employee PRSI before 1 October = 4.20% × W - credit(W)
employee PRSI from 1 October   = 4.35% × W - credit(W)
```

For `W > 424`, there is no credit and the percentages apply to all pay. For `W <= 352`, employee PRSI is nil. Employer PRSI is 9.00%/9.15% where `W <= 552`, and 11.25%/11.40% where `W > 552`. Thus:

```text
annual employee PRSI = 39 × old weekly employee charge
                     + 13 × new weekly employee charge

annual employer PRSI = 39 × old weekly employer charge
                     + 13 × new weekly employer charge
```

For earnings outside the credit band, the 39/13 blended employee rate is **4.2375%**. The corresponding blended employer rates are **9.0375%** below/equal to EUR 552 weekly and **11.2875%** above EUR 552 weekly. These blends are consequences of this stated weekly-pay convention, not statutory standalone rates.

### MyFutureFund

The baseline assumes the employee stays enrolled and has no other payroll pension. All five examples are automatically enrolled: section 50 of the Act uses **not less than EUR 20,000**, notwithstanding the conflicting “more than” shorthand on one DSP campaign page.

For each eligible payroll, employee and employer each contribute 1.5% of that payroll's gross and the State contributes 0.5%. Contributions apply through the entire week in which cumulative gross first exceeds EUR 80,000 and are zero from the next week. With constant weekly salary:

```text
contributory weeks = min(52, ceil(80,000 / W))
contributory gross = contributory weeks × W
employee MFF = employer MFF = 1.5% × contributory gross
State top-up = 0.5% × contributory gross
```

Employee MFF reduces cash take-home but not PAYE, USC, or PRSI. Employer MFF increases employer cost. The State top-up belongs to the retirement account but is neither employee take-home cash nor employer cost.

## 5. Annualisation limitations and unresolved variables

- PAYE and USC normally operate cumulatively using the employee's Revenue Payroll Notification. The annual formulas reproduce the full-year outcome for stable facts and one employment, but a Week 1 basis, late employment, multiple jobs, refunds, or an updated RPN changes deductions during the year.
- PRSI is not an annual assessment. Weekly/fortnightly/monthly thresholds apply to each pay period, and payroll rounds per period. A different frequency can change the low-income credit and cent-level total.
- The 1 October PRSI change must not be replaced blindly with one annual statutory rate. The displayed 39/13 blend is valid only for the stated 52-Friday-pay convention.
- MyFutureFund is also payroll-timed. Because the breach payroll contributes in full, its effective contributory gross can exceed EUR 80,000 and depends on pay frequency and bonuses. The EUR 80,769.23 high-salary result is specific to equal weekly pay.
- Automatic-enrolment status depends on age, pay history, other employments, and whether a qualifying pension contribution already runs through payroll. The calculations deliberately fix age 30, one job, continued participation, and no other pension. Opt-out, suspension, delayed enrolment, or a qualifying employer scheme would change both take-home and employer cost.
- Employer PRSI already includes the 1% National Training Fund levy; it is not added again. No other universal employer payroll percentage was identified for this ordinary Class A case.

## 6. Evidence assessment

**Strongest evidence.** Revenue's dated 2026 tables directly state the PAYE bands, credits, USC bands, and exemption. DSP's updated Class A page and January 2026 SW14 guide give both sides of PRSI before and after 1 October. The MyFutureFund eligibility boundary comes from section 50 of the enacted Act; official worked examples expressly show the breach-payroll overshoot, while Revenue's December 2025 Pensions Manual expressly denies participant tax relief.

**Weakest evidence / modelling sensitivity.** Gross annual salary alone cannot determine exact PRSI or MyFutureFund totals because both depend on pay frequency and pay dates. The 52-Friday-pay convention is transparent and reproducible but is an assumption, not a universal Irish annual payroll rule. MyFutureFund participation also cannot be inferred from gross alone; age, existing payroll pension coverage, enrolment timing, and opt-out status remain employee-specific. One DSP campaign page's “more than EUR 20,000” wording conflicts with the Act's “not less than” test; this dossier follows the enacted Act and separately discloses the conflict.
