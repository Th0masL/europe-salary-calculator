# Czech Republic employee payroll and employer cost, 2026 — independent research dossier

**All sources accessed:** 2026-10-04
**Currency:** Czech koruna (CZK)
**Result type:** 2026 monthly payroll deductions, annual personal-income-tax result, and employer cost.

## 1. Reproducible case

The employee is Czech tax-resident, single, childless, in ordinary full-year private employment, with no other income, benefits, deductions, disability, pensioner status, or tax relief. The employee signs the taxpayer declaration and claims only the standard taxpayer credit. The employment is covered by ordinary sickness, pension, unemployment, and public health insurance; it is not rescue/firefighter or statutory “risk employment,” for which higher employer social rates apply.

Annual cash salary `G` is paid monthly as whole koruna: months 1–11 receive `floor(G / 12)` and December receives the remainder. This explicit schedule matters because payroll tax bases and each insurance contribution are rounded monthly, while the social-security maximum is tracked cumulatively through the calendar year.

The worked results show both (a) cash retained from the twelve payrolls and (b) final cash after the annual Income Tax calculation. With the stated facts, the difference is the refund of excess monthly advances caused by monthly base rounding. Actual payroll software may time that refund in the following year.

## 2. Primary official sources

### Income Tax

1. Financial Administration of the Czech Republic, **“Obecné informace — Zaměstnanci, zaměstnavatelé”** (current 2026 guidance): <https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/dane/dane/dan-z-prijmu/zamestnanci-zamestnavatele/obecne-informace>. Exact pinpoints:

## 3. Calculation order and rounding

For month `m`, let gross whole-koruna salary be `g[m]` and cumulative social assessment before the month be `C[m-1]`.

### Payroll Income Tax advance

```text
monthly tax base B[m] = ceil(g[m] / 100) × 100

gross advance = ceil(
    15% × min(B[m], 146,901)
  + 23% × max(B[m] - 146,901, 0)
)

PAYE advance = max(gross advance - 2,570, 0)
```

At year end, with no deductions:

```text
annual base A = floor(G / 100) × 100

annual tax = max(ceil(
    15% × min(A, 1,762,812)
  + 23% × max(A - 1,762,812, 0)
) - 30,840, 0)
```

The 23% rate is marginal: it never applies to the full base merely because the threshold is crossed.

### Social security

```text
remaining cap = max(2,350,416 - C[m-1], 0)
social base S[m] = min(g[m], remaining cap)

employee social[m] = ceil(7.1% × S[m])
employer social[m] = ceil(24.8% × S[m])
C[m] = C[m-1] + S[m]
```

After the cumulative annual base reaches CZK 2,350,416, both ordinary employee and employer social contributions are zero for later salary in that year. Monthly rounding means annual contributions need not equal exactly 7.1% or 24.8% of the capped annual base.

### Health insurance

```text
health total[m] = ceil(13.5% × max(g[m], 22,400))
employee health[m] = ceil(health total[m] / 3)
employer health[m] = health total[m] - employee health[m]
```

The minimum-base expression is shown for completeness. It does not bind any worked example. There is no maximum health base.

### Net and employer cost

```text
payroll cash = G - monthly PAYE advances
                 - employee social - employee health

final cash after annual tax = G - annual tax
                                - employee social - employee health

quantified employer cost floor = G + employer social + employer health
```

Statutory employer accident insurance must be added to the cost floor at the employer's actual activity rate and quarterly rounding. It is not an employee deduction.

```text
month 6 social base = 2,350,416 - 5 × 416,666 = 267,086

                + ceil(7.1% × 267,086)
                = 5 × 29,584 + 18,964 = 166,884
employer social = 5 × 103,334 + 66,238 = 582,908
months 7–12 social = 0
```

## 5. Universal rules, employer-specific items, and limitations

- The 15%/23% Income Tax rates, CZK 1,762,812 annual threshold, standard taxpayer credit, ordinary social rates, CZK 2,350,416 social ceiling, health rules, and rounding rules are national statutory rules.
- Employer social rates of 29.8% for specified rescue/fire employees and 27.8% for statutory risk employment are exceptions. The selected ordinary office employment uses 24.8%.
- The health minimum base can matter for part-time work, unpaid leave, or low monthly earnings, with statutory exceptions for some state-insured persons and other cases. It does not affect these salaries because every monthly gross exceeds CZK 22,400.
- Health insurance has no maximum. At high salary, health continues on the entire gross after the annual social ceiling has been reached.
- Accident insurance is mandatory but employer-specific. The rate follows the employer's predominant economic activity, not merely this employee's job title. It cannot be inferred from the facts supplied and is deliberately not invented.
- The worked payroll assumes one employer for the whole calendar year. Multiple concurrent employers complicate enforcement/refund of the annual social maximum; mid-year employment changes also alter which month reaches it.
- Monthly payroll rounding makes a simplistic calculation using annual percentages differ by several koruna. The tables preserve the stated monthly sequence and show the annual Income Tax reconciliation separately.

## 6. Evidence assessment

**Strongest evidence.** The Financial Administration's current 2026 employer page states the average-wage derivation, both tax thresholds, both rates, the taxpayer credit, and monthly/annual rounding in one place. MPSV and ČSSZ independently give the exact 2026 social maximum and ČSSZ supplies the employee/employer rate split. VZP directly states the health split, minimum, rounding, and absence of a ceiling.

**Weakest evidence / deliberate non-quantification.** The employer accident premium is legally mandatory, but neither gross salary nor “office employment” identifies the employer's predominant CZ-NACE activity. The official administrator also confirms that the social ceiling does not cap its base. Without the actual employer classification and workforce data, a numeric accident charge would be unsupported, so employer totals are explicitly floors plus that unresolved premium.
