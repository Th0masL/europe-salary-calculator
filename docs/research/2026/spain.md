# Spain employee payroll and income tax, 2026 — independent research dossier

**Access date for every source:** 2026-10-04
**Currency:** euro (EUR)
**Scope:** a 40-year-old Spanish tax resident, single, no children or other dependants, no disability, no other income, and no deductions or credits arising from housing, investment, donations, union/professional fees, mobility, or personal circumstances. The employee has an ordinary, full-time, indefinite private-sector contract and receives only regular cash salary.

This is a clean-room reconstruction from the official sources listed below. It calculates final annual IRPF liability, not payroll withholding. Spanish withholding is a prepayment calculated under its own procedure and can differ from the final return.

## 1. Reproducible location and employment facts

IRPF is not fully uniform across Spain: an autonomous community supplies the regional scale and can change personal/family minima and deductions. A result described simply as “Spain” would therefore be misleading. This dossier selects a taxpayer resident throughout 2026 in the **city and Community of Madrid**. Madrid is chosen because its consolidated regional tax law states both its general scale and its own taxpayer minimum in one official text.

For social-security calculations, the employee is fixed as:

- contribution group **7, Auxiliares Administrativos**;
- exclusively office-based work, occupation code **a** in the occupational-accident tariff;
- salary earned evenly in 12 months, with any extra pays prorated into those months; and
- no overtime, benefits in kind, salary sacrifice, special professional category, or excluded remuneration.

Those facts are assumptions needed to turn monthly statutory rules into one reproducible annual calculation. They are not universal Spanish defaults.

## 4. Social-security calculation

### 4.1 Base and regular rates

For the selected group 7 employee:

```text
annual minimum = 12 × 1,424.40 = 17,092.80
annual maximum M = 12 × 5,101.20 = 61,214.40
B = min(max(annual gross G, 17,092.80), 61,214.40)
```

Every worked salary is above the group-7 minimum. The rates on `B` are:

| Contribution | Employee | Employer |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common contingencies | 4.70% | 23.60% |
| Unemployment, ordinary indefinite contract | 1.55% | 5.50% |
| Vocational training | 0.10% | 0.60% |
| FOGASA | — | 0.20% |
| Intergenerational Equity Mechanism (MEI) | 0.15% | 0.75% |
| Occupational accident, office code `a` assumption | — | 1.50% |
| **Regular total** | **6.50%** | **32.15%** |

Thus regular employee contributions are `6.50% × B`, and regular employer contributions are `32.15% × B`.

### 4.2 Solidarity contribution above the maximum

Order PJC/297/2026 Article 17 gives monthly bands. Because pay is assumed even in all 12 months, the exact annual equivalents are:

```text
t1 = min(max(G - M, 0), 0.10M)             [61,214.40 to 67,335.84]
t2 = min(max(G - 1.10M, 0), 0.40M)         [67,335.84 to 91,821.60]
t3 = max(G - 1.50M, 0)                      [above 91,821.60]

employee solidarity = 0.19%t1 + 0.21%t2 + 0.24%t3
employer solidarity = 0.96%t1 + 1.04%t2 + 1.22%t3
```

The underlying statutory total rates are 1.15%, 1.25%, and 1.46%; their allocations are respectively 0.96/0.19, 1.04/0.21, and 1.22/0.24 percentage points employer/employee. Uneven monthly remuneration would require a month-by-month calculation and may not equal this annual shortcut.

## 5. IRPF calculation

### 5.1 Employment-income base

Let `SS` be regular employee contributions plus employee solidarity contribution.

```text
RNT-for-threshold = G - SS
general expense = 2,000
Article 20 reduction R:
  7,302                                           if RNT <= 14,852
  7,302 - 1.75 × (RNT - 14,852)                  if 14,852 < RNT <= 17,673.52
  2,364.34 - 1.14 × (RNT - 17,673.52)            if 17,673.52 < RNT < 19,747.50
  0                                               otherwise

general liquidable base BL = max(G - SS - 2,000 - R, 0)
```

The Article 20 reduction also requires non-work income not exceeding EUR 6,500; the model has none. `RNT-for-threshold` excludes the Article 19.2(f) EUR 2,000 expense, as the statute specifies for this test.

### 5.2 National and Madrid scales

| National base slice | Rate |
|---:|---:|
| 0–12,450 | 9.50% |
| 12,450–20,200 | 12.00% |
| 20,200–35,200 | 15.00% |
| 35,200–60,000 | 18.50% |
| 60,000–300,000 | 22.50% |
| over 300,000 | 24.50% |

| Madrid base slice | Rate |
|---:|---:|
| 0–13,362.22 | 8.50% |
| 13,362.22–19,004.63 | 10.70% |
| 19,004.63–35,425.68 | 12.80% |
| 35,425.68–57,320.40 | 17.40% |
| over 57,320.40 | 20.50% |

Define `T_state(x)` and `T_Madrid(x)` as tax from the respective progressive tables. For an under-65 taxpayer:

```text
national quota = T_state(BL) - T_state(5,550)
Madrid quota   = T_Madrid(BL) - T_Madrid(5,956.65)
```

Here `T_state(5,550) = 527.25` and `T_Madrid(5,956.65) = 506.31525`. This treatment follows the statutory/AEAT method: personal minima are taxed at the first relevant rates and the resulting amount is subtracted; they are not simply deducted from `BL`.

Madrid's consolidated law contains circumstance-based regional deductions after the scale/minimum provisions. None is treated as a general wage-earner allowance. On the selected facts—age 40, city of Madrid residence, no children or dependants, disability, housing transaction or rent claim, education cost, donation, investment, self-employment, or other qualifying event—the modeled regional deductions are **zero**. National circumstance-based deductions are likewise zero. This is a profile result, not a universal statement about available deductions.

### 5.3 2026 low-employment-income credit

With zero other income, the additional-provision-61 credit is:

```text
D = 590.89                                      if G <= 17,094
D = 590.89 - 0.20 × (G - 17,094)               if 17,094 < G <= 20,048.45
D = 0                                           if G > 20,048.45
```

It is legally capped by the quota attributable to qualifying net work income. The cap does not bind in the EUR 20,000 example. Final modeled IRPF is `national quota + Madrid quota - D`.

## 7. Evidence limits and unresolved matters

- **Strongest evidence:** Order PJC/297/2026 is a single, final BOE instrument expressly governing 2026 and supplies the bases, ordinary rates, MEI, unemployment, training, FOGASA, and exact three-band solidarity split. The current consolidated national and Madrid statutes directly supply the two IRPF scales and minima.
- **Weakest/model-dependent evidence:** the 1.50% occupational-accident rate is legally exact only if the employee truly performs exclusively office work within tariff occupation `a`. Another occupation or applicable CNAE produces a different employer cost.
- The selected contribution group is a fact assumption. A different job classification can change the minimum base, although it would not change any of the five examples because all five salaries exceed the selected minimum and the same general maximum applies.
- Monthly timing matters. Bonuses, non-even pay, absences, multiple concurrent employers, or employment for fewer than 12 months require monthly bases and solidarity bands rather than the annualized shortcut.
- Madrid deductions are deliberately zero because the facts needed to claim one are absent. Eligibility for rent, birth/adoption, disability, education, donations, investment, or other national/regional relief would change IRPF.
- No church-tax analogue exists in this calculation. Spanish IRPF's tax-allocation choices do not add tax liability.
- Collective-agreement costs, employer pension/insurance obligations, and payroll administration costs are not statutory universal payroll percentages and cannot be calculated from the stated facts.
- This dossier does not calculate withholding. A payslip may therefore show a different IRPF retention even where the final annual liability above is correct under the stated facts.
