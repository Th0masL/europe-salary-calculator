How it works. For each location the data gives total employer cost and net pay at a few gross-salary points; the tool interpolates linearly between them and inverts the relationship, so you can fix the employer budget, the gross, or the target net and solve for the rest. Amounts can be displayed in EUR or USD; the USD view uses the latest available ECB reference rate or your manual override.

Calculation methodology. Results are computed from published tax rates in the country modules under tools/calc/, with no EOR vendor calculator in the live calculation. The generated dataset samples every €5,000 of gross salary from €20,000 through €600,000. European rows use a representative 40-year-old single employee with no children or dependants; country pages name the fixed location and employer assumptions used where national rules alone are insufficient. Read the country-by-country calculation reference.

US cities (🇺🇸) are state-level — 11 of them (Seattle→WA, San Francisco→CA, New York→NY, Austin→TX, Atlanta→GA, Miami→FL, Chicago→IL, Los Angeles→CA, Boston→MA, Washington→DC, Denver→CO). Their results use 2026 federal brackets, FICA (with the Social Security cap), and state tax including NYC local tax, with no EOR vendor involved. Mandatory state paid-leave payroll charges are included for the documented representative larger-employer scenario; benefits and workers' compensation remain excluded. Cost of living stays city-specific.

Figures are estimates for comparison only — they ignore circumstances outside that fixed profile, alternative regional or employer scenarios, benefits, bonuses, and currency fluctuations. Standard reliefs belonging to the stated profile are included. All rows use 2026 rules. Do not use them as precise payroll or budgeting figures. “After living costs” subtracts the estimated annual cost of living for a single person (capital city for countries, the city itself for US entries).
